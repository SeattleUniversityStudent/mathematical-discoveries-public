## Statement

In global Fibonacci Nim a position is (a,b,c;r): three heaps of nonnegative sizes and a bound r. A move removes t counters from one heap, with 1 <= t <= r and t at most the heap size, and the next bound is 2t. The player who cannot move loses. A P-position loses for the player to move; an N-position wins for the player to move.

With first bound r = 1:

- (6,6,6;1) is a P-position, while (6;1) (one heap of 6, written (0,0,6;1)) is an N-position;
- (20,20,20;1) is an N-position, while (20;1) (written (0,0,20;1)) is a P-position.

So deleting two equal heaps can change the outcome, in either direction. The all-n classification of the positions (n,n,n;1) is not obtained, and none is claimed. No infinite family, no claim of least size beyond what PROOF.md states, and no Sprague-Grundy statement is part of the formally checked claim.

## Prior work

The game is defined and studied in Urban Larsson and Simon Rubinstein-Salzedo, Global Fibonacci Nim, arXiv:1509.08527 (2015); journal version: International Journal of Game Theory 47 (2018) 595-611, DOI 10.1007/s00182-017-0574-x (the journal text was not read for this record, only its metadata). The finite examples here are diagnostics and priority is undetermined.

## Limitations

The formal statements concern four specific positions. They do not settle which (n,n,n;1) are P-positions, and they do not formalize the written note's recurrence or tables. The replay program prints no per-declaration log; see FORMAL/verification/PROVENANCE.md for what the replay evidence does and does not show.
